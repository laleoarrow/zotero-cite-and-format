# Review Manuscripts

Use this reference for narrative reviews, state-of-the-art reviews, perspectives, primers, and review-style major sections inside Word manuscripts.

## Core Principle
Review prose should read as synthesis, not as notes from the writing desk.

- Avoid colloquial metaphors.
- Avoid sentences that narrate the author's thought process or explain what the author is trying to do.
- Prefer review-style definition sentences, scope-limiting sentences, contrast sentences, and synthesis transitions.
- Build the argument at section level. Do not rely on stylistic flourish to substitute for structure.

## Review Introduction Gate
For review introductions, the default flow is:
1. clinical or scientific problem
2. organizing distinction or classification frame
3. why that frame is insufficient on its own
4. the specific lens of the review
5. scope boundary

Do not open with chatty scene-setting, essayistic throat-clearing, or stage-direction prose such as:
- what the author wants to explore
- what the reader should now consider
- why the author will next turn to a topic

Instead, prefer sentences that define the field, delimit the problem, or mark a mechanistic transition.

## Review Section Rhythm
Default cadence for a review paragraph:
- positioning sentence
- evidence synthesis
- limitation or heterogeneity boundary
- forward link or synthesis implication

Do not fall into paper-by-paper recital unless the user explicitly wants a historical survey.
Organize by question, mechanism, phenotype, syndrome, or clinical decision point.

## Review Prose Filters
- Keep sentences direct and journal-facing.
- Prefer precise qualifiers such as `is associated with`, `supports`, `is consistent with`, `has been linked to`, `remains uncertain`.
- Avoid overclaiming and avoid false symmetry. If evidence is uneven, say so directly.
- Do not pad a review section with generic burden language after the first positioning paragraph.
- Do not preview every downstream subsection in the introduction. End with the real scope and value proposition instead.

## Review Formatting Gates
- If the journal requires key messages, highlights, or summary boxes, preserve their exact heading structure and citation policy.
- Avoid citations in the abstract or key messages unless the journal explicitly requires or tolerates them.
- Evidence tables in reviews should be selective and claim-defining; they do not need to reproduce every screened paper.
- Keep subsection titles conceptual and stable; avoid table-of-contents headings that merely repeat the manuscript outline without adding meaning.

## Interaction with Common Formatting
- `manuscript-formatting.md` remains the common Word-formatting base.
- This file adds review-specific prose and section gates on top of that base.
- Do not treat `manuscript-formatting.md` alone as sufficient for a review-manuscript task.
